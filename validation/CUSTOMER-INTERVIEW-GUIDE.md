# Customer interview guide

Prepared 2026-11-20. Updated 2026-11-20 with Gate 0 answers. Status: draft. No interviews have been scheduled and no one has been contacted. Customer outreach is NOT AUTHORIZED. The interview lead is not decided (UNKNOWN, see GATE-0-OPEN-ITEMS.md section 2).

## Gate 0 safeguards that apply to every interview

CONFIRMED at Gate 0. CCS has existing med spa clients, and they are a potential warm-contact pool. To keep agency goodwill from being read as product demand:

1. Maximum 5 warm contacts among 20 interviews.
2. Source channel and relationship are recorded for every participant before the call starts.
3. Warm and cold results are tallied separately in every table.
4. A positive comment is not demand. Do not report "they liked it" as a finding.
5. What counts: money they already spend on this job, a problem they describe without being asked, and whether the same problem repeats across participants.
6. If the interviewer is the participant's own CCS account manager, record that. It is the most biased setting there is.

## Purpose

Find out how independent med spas actually handle a new inquiry, what it costs them today, and whether the gap is painful enough to pay for. The interview is evidence gathering. It is not a sales call.

## Ground rules

1. No pitch in the first two thirds of the call. The offer is shown only in section G.
2. Ask about the last real event, not about opinions. "Walk me through the last inquiry that came in" beats "Do you struggle with follow-up?"
3. Never suggest the answer. If they do not raise lead follow-up on their own, record that. It is a result.
4. Ask for numbers and then ask how they know. "About 40 leads a month" from memory and "42, I'm looking at the report" are different quality.
5. Silence is fine. Let them fill it.
6. Record only with permission, stated at the start. In states that require all-party consent, a verbal yes on the recording is the minimum. If they decline, take notes.
7. Do not collect patient names or patient details. If they share any, leave it out of the notes.
8. 30 minutes. End on time.

## Opening (2 minutes)

"Thanks for making the time. I'm talking to about 20 med spa owners and managers to understand how new inquiries get handled, from the first call or form to a booked consult. I'm not selling anything today. There are no right answers, and I'm most interested in what really happens on a busy day. Is it ok if I record this so I don't have to type the whole time? It stays with me."

## A. Business context (3 minutes)

1. Tell me a bit about the practice. How long have you been open, and who's on the team?
2. Which treatments bring in most new patients?
3. What's a new patient worth to you over the first year, roughly? How do you know?
4. What do you use for booking and charting? How long have you been on it?

## B. Where inquiries come from (4 minutes)

5. Where do new inquiries come from right now? Rank them if you can.
6. About how many come in each month? Where do you see that number?
7. Are you running paid ads? Who runs them, and what do you spend a month?
8. Which source gives you the best patients? Which gives you the worst? How can you tell?

## C. The last inquiry, step by step (7 minutes)

This is the core of the interview. Slow down here.

9. Think about the last new inquiry that came in through a form or an ad. What happened first? Who saw it, and where?
10. How long until someone replied? How did they reply (call, text, email)?
11. What happened if the person didn't answer? How many more tries, and over how long?
12. Where is that inquiry written down right now? Can you see every open inquiry in one place?
13. Now the phone. What happens when a call comes in and the front desk is with a patient? After hours?
14. What about Instagram or Facebook messages? Who answers those, and from what device?
15. When someone says "let me think about it" after a consult, what happens next?
16. Who is responsible for all of this? What else is that person doing at the same time?

Listen for: sticky notes, spreadsheets, personal phones, "whoever's free", "we try to", "I do it at night". Write the exact words.

## D. What it costs today (4 minutes)

17. What software are you paying for each month? Let's list it: booking, texting, reviews, marketing, phone. Roughly what does each cost?
18. Do you pay an agency? What for, and how much? What's included?
19. Have you used an answering service, a virtual receptionist or an AI receptionist? What happened?
20. Does your booking system have its own texting, lead or AI front-desk feature? Do you use it? Why or why not?
21. Are you in a contract with any of these? When does it end?

## E. Pain and priority (4 minutes)

22. If you could fix three things about running the practice this quarter, what would they be?

Record the answer exactly and in order before saying anything else. This is the unprompted problem ranking the scorecard uses. Only after they finish:

23. Where does handling new inquiries sit against those three?
24. How many inquiries do you think never get a proper reply? What makes you think that?
25. What have you already tried to fix this? What did it cost, and why did it stop?
26. What would have to be true for you to trust a new tool with your leads?

## F. Reporting and attribution (2 minutes)

27. Can you tell which ad or source produced the patients who actually showed up? How?
28. What number do you wish you could see every Monday that you can't see today?

## G. Offer reaction and willingness to pay (4 minutes)

Only now describe the offer, in two sentences:

"Here's what I'm considering building. A service that makes sure every form, ad lead and missed call gets a text reply within a few minutes, keeps following up until they book or say stop, sends them to your existing booking page, and gives you a weekly report of leads, response time, booked and showed by source. It sits beside your booking system and doesn't replace it."

29. What's your first reaction?
30. What would worry you about it?
31. What's missing that would make it a must-have?

Price sensitivity. Ask all four, in this order, before showing any price:

32. At what monthly price would this be so cheap you'd doubt it works?
33. At what monthly price would it be a bargain?
34. At what monthly price would it start to feel expensive, but you'd still consider it?
35. At what monthly price is it too expensive to consider at all?

Then show the three packages from PILOT-OFFER.md, same prices to everyone. Show the package table only, as a concept to react to. Do not mention a money-back guarantee, a price lock or any overage rate: the first two are CONDITIONAL and the third is DEFERRED. Do not describe the product as HIPAA compliant or compliant with any law. If asked, the accurate answer is that compliance review is still to be done and no pilot starts before it is.

36. Which of these would you pick, if any? Why that one?
37. What would you remove to pay less? What would you pay more to add?
38. Would a setup fee change your answer? Would a 12-month commitment for a discount appeal, or put you off?
39. Who else would need to say yes before you bought this?

## H. Close (1 minute)

40. If I run a 90-day paid pilot of this, do you want me to come back to you? (Record yes, maybe or no. A yes here is interest, not a sale.)
41. Who else should I talk to? Any owner you know who'd have a strong opinion?
42. Anything I should have asked and didn't?

## Front-desk variant

When the interviewee is a front-desk lead and not the owner, keep sections C and E, shorten D to tools they personally use, skip G's price questions, and add:

- What's the most stressful part of your day?
- When a lead comes in while you're checking someone out, what do you do?
- What do you wish the owner understood about the phones?
- If a tool sent the first text for you, what would you want it to say, and what should it never say?

## Reading the results honestly

- Count what people did, not what they said they would do. "I'd pay $300" is weak. "I pay Podium $450 and I'm annoyed" is strong.
- A stated willingness to pay is not a sale. The only real price evidence in this phase is a paid pilot.
- Separate warm and cold contacts in every tally. Gate 2 has to pass on the cold-only tally as well as overall.
- Positive comments about the idea are not evidence of demand and are never reported as such. Report three things: what they spend today on this job, what they described unprompted, and how many participants described the same thing.
- A problem counts as repeated when at least 5 participants describe it unprompted and at least 3 of them are cold contacts (ASSUMPTION, to be agreed with the other thresholds).
- Evidence from WARM-1 participants shows what existing clients value in CCS. Label it that way.
- If fewer than half put lead follow-up in their top three unprompted, that is a stop-or-reposition signal under DECISION-GATES.md, even if they liked the offer when it was described.
- Watch for a different problem that keeps coming up. If 8 of 20 raise the same thing nobody asked about, write it down in the scorecard. That is how the real opportunity usually shows itself.
