# US Independent Med Spa Market Overview (background for a lead management / appointment conversion / follow-up SaaS)

Research date: all URLs accessed 2026-11-20.

Labelling convention used on every line:
- **FACT** = a figure or statement published by the named source (the source may itself be a survey or vendor dataset; the publication year and data year are stated).
- **ESTIMATE** = a projection, model, or paid-report teaser figure; the estimator is named.
- **ASSUMPTION** = my own inference or arithmetic. Not sourced.
- **[VENDOR]** = published by a company that sells software or marketing services to med spas. Treat as directional.
- **[SNIPPET]** = seen only in a search-result summary; the underlying page was not opened and read in full. Lower confidence; verify before quoting.

Important framing caveat: the most recent AmSpa Medical Spa State of the Industry Report found is the **2024 edition** (survey fielded Sept 2023 to March 2024, figures describe calendar 2023). AmSpa publishes roughly every two years. I found no 2025 or 2026 edition as of 2026-11-20, so the core "count, revenue, staffing, ownership" figures are about three years old.

## 1. How many med spas are there in the US, how fast is the count growing, and what share are single-location independents vs multi-location groups?

### Takeaway
The last hard count is 10,488 US med spas at end of 2023 (AmSpa 2024 report), up 17.9% from 8,899 in 2022, with 81% single-location and only 3% PE-owned. A 2026 journal-attributed forecast puts the count near 13,000 by end of 2026, but no AmSpa count newer than 2023 was found.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report, data for 2023; recap published 2024-11-06): med spa locations rose from 8,899 (2022) to 10,488 (2023). — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 81% of med spas are single-location. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): multi-location med spas average nine locations, up from six in the 2022 report. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): private equity owns 3% of med spas, unchanged from the 2022 report; reported M&A activity among respondents was "surprisingly low". — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 18% of med spas opened in 2023 (17% in the 2022 report). — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report methodology): online survey of US med spa owners fielded Sept 2023 to March 2024 with partner Gordian Solutions Group. [SNIPPET] — [AmSpa press release, 2024](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/amspas-2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-report-shows-medical-aesthetics-continues-steady-growth-after-pandemic/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Stacker / Skin Clique Academy article, published 2026-10-12, citing AmSpa): 5,431 med spas in 2018; 1,589 net additions in 2023, a 17.86% increase. Note the article is produced by an injector training company. — [KEYT/Stacker, Oct 2026](https://keyt.com/stacker-health/2026/10/12/more-med-spas-than-mcdonalds-2027-forecasts-say-yes/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- ESTIMATE (attributed by the Stacker article to a 2026 Aesthetic Surgery Journal report; primary paper not read by me): about 13,000 US med spas by year-end 2026. — [KEYT/Stacker, Oct 2026](https://keyt.com/stacker-health/2026/10/12/more-med-spas-than-mcdonalds-2027-forecasts-say-yes/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Xite, M&A advisor, published on AmSpa 2026-05-15): "over 90% of med spas are independently owned". This is an advisor's characterization with no stated methodology, and uses "independent" (not chain/PE-owned) rather than AmSpa's "single-location" definition. — [AmSpa/Xite, May 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/med-spa-ma-and-private-sales-a-look-back-at-2025-and-what-lies-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (Zenoti 2026 Benchmark Report, medspa edition, calendar 2025 data from Zenoti customers, which skew multi-location): medspa "center growth" of 18% in 2025. — [Zenoti 2026 benchmark](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/the-2026-beauty-and-wellness-benchmark-report-medspa-edition) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa research page, dated 2026): industry described as "$17B+", growing "$1B+" per year. The page does not tie the figure to a specific data year. — [AmSpa Research](https://americanmedspa.org/resources/med-spa-statistics) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Guidepoint Qsight / Skytale report "Defining the Future of Aesthetics", reported 2026-01-09): US aesthetics practice spending rose 4% in the first three quarters of 2025 to $21 billion. This covers aesthetics practices broadly (med spas plus derm/plastics), not med spas only. — [Beauty Independent, Jan 2026](https://www.beautyindependent.com/?p=181586) (accessed 2026-11-20)

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION (my arithmetic): 81% of 10,488 is about 8,500 single-location med spas at end of 2023. If the 13,000 forecast for end-2026 holds and the 81% share is unchanged, that is about 10,500 single-location businesses. The 81% share is three years old and consolidation has continued, so treat about 8,500 to 10,500 as the plausible range of single-location med spas, not a measured number.
- ASSUMPTION (my arithmetic): the remaining 19% (about 2,000 locations in 2023) at an average of nine locations per group implies only a few hundred multi-location operators. The AmSpa "81%" may be a share of survey respondents (businesses) rather than of locations; the recap does not say, and the two readings give different answers.
- ASSUMPTION (my arithmetic): implied count growth from 10,488 (end 2023) to about 13,000 (end 2026 forecast) is about 7.4% a year, well below the 17.9% recorded for 2023. If accurate, new-opening growth has slowed sharply.
- ASSUMPTION: 10,488 x $1,398,833 average revenue is about $14.7B, below AmSpa's "$17B+" headline. The gap is unexplained in the sources I read; the two figures may use different years or scopes.

### Gaps
- No AmSpa count newer than 2023. No 2025 or 2026 State of the Industry edition was found.
- An AmSpa August 2024 press release reportedly cited a much smaller industry revenue figure ($1.4 billion) that conflicts with $17B [SNIPPET, not opened]; likely a typo or different scope, unresolved.
- A third-party location-mapping dataset (Orbital, mid-2026) claims its own med spa and chain counts, but the page redirected and could not be read. Not used.
- The primary Aesthetic Surgery Journal paper behind the 13,000 forecast was not located or read.
- No source gave the number of distinct multi-location operators, or the share of locations (as opposed to businesses) held by chains.

## 2. Average and median revenue per location, staff count, patient visits, spend per visit, profit margins

### Takeaway
The AmSpa 2024 report gives an average of about $1.40M revenue per med spa, 245 visits a month, $527 per visit and eight employees at a single-location practice. No independent median revenue was found; the only median ($1.86M) comes from Zenoti's customer base, which skews larger.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report, 2023 data): average annual revenue $1,398,833, up from $1,307,587 in the 2022 report (about +7%). — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): average spend per visit $527, versus $536 in the 2022 report. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): average of 245 patient visits per month, described as nearly unchanged from 2018. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): single-location practices average eight employees; multi-location practices average 12 employees at their primary location (16 in the 2022 report, 15 in the 2019 report). — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 73% of patients on average are repeat patients (65% in the 2022 report); 89% of patients are female; female patients are 54% aged 35-54, 24% aged 55+, 22% aged 18-34. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 84% of owners expected revenue to rise in 2024; 54% of those expected at least +10%. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (Growth99 2026 State of Aesthetic & Elective Wellness Marketing Report, summarized on AmSpa 2026-01-09): average profit margin 38%; average med spa revenue $1.39M (citing AmSpa); average visit value $527. The article does not say what kind of margin (gross, EBITDA, net) or give a sample size. — [AmSpa/Growth99, Jan 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/the-marketing-investment-gap-why-half-of-med-spa-practices-are-falling-behind-and-the-other-half-are-pulling-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (Zenoti 2026 Benchmark, calendar 2025, North American Zenoti customers, no sample size given): revenue per location median $1,860,000; 75th percentile $2,340,000; 90th percentile $4,250,000. — [Zenoti 2026 benchmark](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/the-2026-beauty-and-wellness-benchmark-report-medspa-edition) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (Zenoti 2026 Benchmark, 2025 data): average ticket median $216; 75th percentile $346; 90th percentile $484 (90th percentile was $500 in 2023 and $454 in 2024). — [Zenoti 2026 benchmark](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/the-2026-beauty-and-wellness-benchmark-report-medspa-edition) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (Zenoti 2026 Benchmark, 2025 data): staff utilization median 38%, 75th percentile 56%, 90th percentile 80%; cancellation rate improved from 16% to 14%. — [Zenoti 2026 benchmark](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/the-2026-beauty-and-wellness-benchmark-report-medspa-edition) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Xite, 2026-05-15, describing 2025 deals): EBITDA multiples of 3x-6x for practices under $4M revenue, 5x-8x for $4M-$20M, 7x-12x for over $20M regional platforms. — [AmSpa/Xite, May 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/med-spa-ma-and-private-sales-a-look-back-at-2025-and-what-lies-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION (my arithmetic): 245 visits x $527 x 12 months is about $1.55M, about 11% above the reported $1.40M average revenue. The AmSpa averages are not internally reconcilable from the recap alone (different respondent bases per question is the likely reason).

- ASSUMPTION (my arithmetic): eight employees and $1.40M revenue implies about $175K revenue per employee at a single-location practice.

## 3. Who owns them (physician, NP, RN, non-clinical entrepreneur) and what does that mean for software buying decisions?

### Takeaway
Two thirds of single-owner med spas are owned by people who are not physicians or surgeons, and nurse practitioners have nearly caught up with physicians as the largest owner group (AmSpa 2024 report). The exact 2024 split by NP, RN, PA and entrepreneur was not available in free sources.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 67% of single-owner practices are owned by non-MDs/surgeons. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): NPs "have nearly pulled even with MDs and surgeons" as the group with the highest ownership share, a significant increase over the 2022 report. No exact percentage is given. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 81% of single-owner med spas are owned by non-core specialists; 74% of medical directors come from non-core specialties (69% in the 2022 report); 48% of med spas hired a medical director in the past year. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (AmSpa 2022 report, secondary citation) [SNIPPET]: in 2022, 23% of single-owner med spas were NP-owned, up from 11% in 2019; 37% of single private-owner med spas were physician-owned; 63% were owned by non-physicians/non-surgeons. AmSpa's "non-MD/surgeon" category includes NPs, RNs, PAs, entrepreneurs, aestheticians and others. — [ASDS article citing AmSpa 2022](https://asds.net/Portals/0/PDF/asdsa/Trends%20in%20Medical%20Spa%20Statistics%20and%20Patient%20Safety%20Article.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-20, snippet only)
- FACT (AmSpa 2017 survey, historical baseline, published 2017-10-05): nearly 77% of med spas were affiliated with a physician in some way. Too old to describe today's market; included only to show the shift away from physician ownership. — [AmSpa, 2017](https://americanmedspa.org/blog/who-are-the-most-commonly-employed-team-members-in-a-med-spa) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Holland & Knight, 2026-08-31): many states restrict the corporate practice of medicine, so investor-backed groups commonly use an MSO structure in which a management company holds non-clinical assets and a physician-owned entity employs clinicians. — [Holland & Knight, Aug 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/private-equity-investment-in-dermatology-and-medical-spa-platforms) (accessed 2026-11-20)

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION: in a single-location med spa with about eight staff, the owner (most often an NP, RN, PA or non-clinical entrepreneur who is also frequently a working injector) is the economic buyer for software. There is no IT or procurement function. Purchase decisions are likely fast, price-sensitive and driven by peer referral and trade-show exposure. No source measured this directly.
- ASSUMPTION: clinician-owners who treat patients all day have little time to evaluate tools or work a lead list, which supports "done for you" or low-setup positioning. Entrepreneur-owners and MSO-run groups are more likely to think in funnel metrics.
- ASSUMPTION: because the business handles patient data under a medical director, HIPAA posture (BAA, secure messaging) is a likely gating question for any tool that texts or emails leads. No source quantified how often this blocks a purchase.

### Gaps
- Exact 2024-report ownership percentages for NP, RN, PA, physician and non-clinical entrepreneur: not available outside the paid report.
- No survey found on who makes software purchase decisions in med spas, typical sales cycle, or number of tools per practice.

## 4. What segments exist by size and which are realistic buyers of a third-party lead/follow-up tool?

### Takeaway
Sources support a simple structure: a large base of single-location practices (81%, averaging eight staff), a small layer of multi-location groups (averaging nine locations), a handful of PE-backed platforms, and national corporate or franchise chains. No source sizes the "solo injector suite" segment or reports software buying behaviour by segment, so the buyer ranking below is my assumption.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (AmSpa 2024 report): 81% single-location; multi-location operators average nine locations; single-location practices average eight employees; PE owns 3%. — [AmSpa 2024 Executive Report Recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT (Xite, 2026-05-15): named PE-backed platforms include Empower Aesthetics (Shore Capital), Alpha Aesthetics (Thurston Group), Advanced MedAesthetic Partners (Leon Capital) and Well Labs+ (Camino Partners). Operators with three to eight or more sites, centralized administration and standardized procedures draw the most acquirer interest. Add-on acquisitions were the most active deal type in 2025. — [AmSpa/Xite, May 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/med-spa-ma-and-private-sales-a-look-back-at-2025-and-what-lies-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- ESTIMATE [SNIPPET] (Orbital, a data vendor's mid-2026 location classifier; page could not be opened, internal tables reportedly inconsistent): LaserAway about 145 storefronts, corporate-run, backed by Lightyear Capital; Ideal Image about 175 locations; Milan Laser Hair Removal about 380 corporate clinics. — [Orbital chain ranking](https://www.withorbital.com/data/largest-aesthetic-clinic-chains-in-the-us/) (accessed 2026-11-20, snippet only)
- FACT [SNIPPET] (company press release, Jan 2026): VIO Med Spa, a franchise system, operates 64 locations across 20 states. — [VIO Med Spa release](https://www.webull.com/news/14155869426844672) (accessed 2026-11-20, snippet only)
- FACT [SNIPPET] (Improvado research brief, 2026): states that Ideal Image "came apart in 2025". Not verified; conflicts with the Orbital location count above. — [Improvado med spa brief](https://improvado.io/marketing-research/multi-location-healthcare/med-spa-aesthetics) (accessed 2026-11-20, snippet only)
- FACT (Beauty Independent reporting Guidepoint Qsight/Skytale, 2026-01-09): top-tier chains are valued 30% to 100% higher than those without integrated operating systems. — [Beauty Independent, Jan 2026](https://www.beautyindependent.com/?p=181586) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- ESTIMATE [SNIPPET] (Market.us paid-report teaser): large medical spas and chains hold 67.2% of med spa management software spend. Lower confidence. — [Market.us](https://market.us/report/medical-spa-management-software-market/) (accessed 2026-11-20, snippet only)

### Inferences
All of the following segmentation is ASSUMPTION (mine), built on the facts above:
- Segment A, solo injector / suite renter (1-2 people, no front desk): exists in volume but is not sized by any source found. Likely low willingness to pay and low lead volume; owner answers their own phone and DMs. Weak buyer for a paid third-party lead tool; better served by all-in-one booking apps.
- Segment B, single-location practice with roughly 3-10 staff (AmSpa average is eight): the core of the 81%. Has a front desk, runs paid social, and has a visible follow-up gap (see section 5). Most realistic buyer. Decision maker is the owner or a practice manager.
- Segment C, 2-5 location group: fewer accounts but higher contract value, more likely to have a marketing lead or agency, and the profile acquirers pay most for. Realistic buyer; may demand multi-location reporting and integration with the practice management system.
- Segment D, PE-backed platforms and 10+ location groups: centralized call centers and enterprise stacks (Zenoti-class). Long sales cycles, integration and security requirements. Not a realistic early buyer for a new third-party tool.
- Segment E, national corporate chains and franchises (LaserAway, Ideal Image, Milan, VIO, Alchemy 43): corporate-mandated systems; franchisees usually cannot choose their own lead tools. Not realistic buyers except through a corporate deal.

### Gaps
- No count of solo injector suites or of practices by staff-size band.
- Alchemy 43 location count: not found.
- Current LaserAway and Ideal Image location counts from primary sources: not found; Ideal Image's 2025-2026 status is unverified.
- No data on which segments actually buy third-party lead or follow-up tools, churn by segment, or contract values.

## 5. What do med spas spend on software and marketing per month? Where do leads come from? How do they operate day to day?

### Takeaway
Half of med spas spend under $2,500 a month on marketing and only a quarter spend $5,000 or more (Growth99 2026, vendor data), with Instagram and paid social the dominant channels. Leads arrive mostly by phone and are handled by front desk staff, and measured response times range from under 13 minutes to days; no reliable figure exists for total software spend per practice.

### Cited Findings

Marketing spend
- FACT [VENDOR] (Growth99 2026 report, on AmSpa 2026-01-09): 52% of med spa practices invest less than $2,500 per month in marketing; 25% invest $5,000 or more. Sample size not stated. — [AmSpa/Growth99, Jan 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/the-marketing-investment-gap-why-half-of-med-spa-practices-are-falling-behind-and-the-other-half-are-pulling-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (same): benchmark of 5% of revenue on marketing, which at $1.39M average revenue is about $5,800 per month. — [AmSpa/Growth99, Jan 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/the-marketing-investment-gap-why-half-of-med-spa-practices-are-falling-behind-and-the-other-half-are-pulling-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)
- FACT [VENDOR] (same): average cost per lead $39; average new patient acquisition cost $132; average visit value $527. — [AmSpa/Growth99, Jan 2026](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/the-marketing-investment-gap-why-half-of-med-spa-practices-are-falling-behind-and-the-other-half-are-pulling-ahead/) (accessed 2026-11-20)

## 6. Market trends: PE consolidation, GLP-1 weight loss services, regulatory pressure, softening consumer demand

### Takeaway
Demand softening is documented: med spa same-store growth fell below 1% in 2025 (Guidepoint Qsight) and Zenoti shows new guest visits down 11% in 2025, with growth coming from new locations. Consolidation continues but from a low base, GLP-1 revenue growth has collapsed from triple digits to single digits, and state regulators are tightening supervision and deal-review rules.

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION: flat same-store sales plus falling new-patient visits plus continued new openings means more locations are competing for a flat pool of new patients. - ASSUMPTION: tighter supervision and delegation rules raise compliance cost for non-physician owners and make them cautious about any tool that messages patients, so HIPAA and consent handling matter for the product.
